Every sales call at Revryze follows the **DRIVE** framework. Memorize the steps. When a call feels stuck, ask yourself: which step did I skip?

## DRIVE at a glance

- **D — Disarm** — a status-raising opener that drops their guard.
- **R — Reserve the close** — the upfront contract: "if we both agree this is a fit, I can save you more."
- **I — Investigate** — the why behind the why. Get to their emotional motivation.
- **V — Value-stack** — position the founding membership and tier pricing against what they just told you they want.
- **E — Execute** — the close. "Are you opposed to getting set up today?"

---

## D — Disarm

The lead is expecting a pushy salesperson. Disarm means your first twenty seconds prove you're not that. The tone is casual, status-equal, and time-aware.

### The canonical cold-call opener

> "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] over at [Client]. I know I'm giving you a call out of the blue — I just had a quick second between calls setting people up. I wanted to reach out and see what initially got you interested."

Five ingredients, all load-bearing:

1. **"Quick second between calls setting people up"** — you are busy because the brand is wanted. Status-raising.
2. **"Out of the blue"** — you acknowledge the interruption before they can weaponize it.
3. **"Wanted to reach out"** — warm, not scripted.
4. **"What initially got you interested"** — gets them talking about themselves inside 30 seconds. This is the most important question of the call.
5. **First name basis. Always.**

### Never open with "is now a good time?"

"Is now a good time?" is a trained brush-off trigger. Every lead's reflex answer is no. Use time-reciprocity instead:

> "Hey, I don't have much time either. If you give me 90 seconds, I can see if this is even relevant."

You've just matched their urgency, lowered the perceived ask, and gated the whole call to 90 seconds of their time.

### Status self-talk before the call

You're the one with the inventory. You're the one who chose to call them. They're the one who filled out a form asking for this conversation. Sound like that.

## R — Reserve the Close

The upfront contract. You tell them what's going to happen and get their buy-in before the pitch starts. This single move eliminates most end-of-call objections.

### Canonical wording

> "I want to see if this is even relevant to you. And if, by the end of the call, we both agree this is a fit for what you're looking for, I can save you more on our founding memberships. Is that fair?"

What just happened:

- You got a yes. Their first yes of the call.
- You framed the close as mutual — "if we both agree" — not coercive.
- You made the discount contingent on buy-in, not on their pushback.
- You reserved permission to present a price at the end.

Do this on every call — cold and book. **Skipping the upfront contract is the single most common reason a call stalls at pricing.**

## I — Investigate

This is where the sale is actually made. Everything after this stage is just assembly.

### The Why Chain

Ask why. Then ask why again. Keep asking, gently, until you get to the emotional motivation underneath the stated goal. Most pitches die because the rep stopped at the stated goal — "to get in shape" — without finding the emotional weight — "my doctor told me I have to," "my husband is worried," "I turn 40 in six months." **That emotional weight is your closing ammunition.**

Canonical follow-up prompts:

- "Tell me about your current wellness routine."
- "What are you hoping to change?"
- "If you saw yourself 90 days from now looking the way you want, what would that look like?"
- "What's different about this moment — why now, not six months ago?"

### Take notes during Investigate

Type what they say verbatim: "back pain," "no time," "hasn't tried Pilates," "husband turned 50." You'll use their exact words against them at the Execute step. This is the single most under-used technique in sales.

### The core axiom

> **There is always an objection behind the objection.**
>
> When a lead gives you an objection, that's rarely the real one. They're reaching for whatever excuse comes naturally. Your job in Investigate is to surface the real one now, before it hides behind a different excuse at close time.
>
> Example: "I need to check with my spouse" almost always means "it's more than I wanted to spend." Handling the spousal objection without uncovering the financial one wastes the next ten minutes.

### Listen ratio

Aim for **60/40 lead-to-rep talk time.** 40/60 is elite. If you're talking more than 60% of the call, you're pitching at them instead of selling to them. Silence is a tool — when you ask a good question, let it hang. They'll fill the silence with the information you need.

## V — Value-Stack

You now know their goal, their hesitation, and their emotional weight. Pitch the product against that. The pitch is not a monologue about the brand — it is a tailored match between their words and our offer.

### Canonical founding-membership pitch

> "You filled out your info for our founding membership. Here's what that is. Discounted rate because we're in pre-sales. You're charged once, before opening, for your first month only. No enrollment fee. No contract. And there's a grace-period refund once we open — if you hate it, you get your money back. So there's literally zero risk to locking in the discounted rate. You also get priority access and some grab-bag perks, but no one signs up for a free t-shirt. That's the founding membership. Does that make sense?"

Structural notes:

- The **"zero risk"** frame does all the heavy lifting. Hit it clearly and early.
- Downplaying the t-shirt signals you're not here to con them — trust lift.
- "Does that make sense?" harvests their next yes before you move to pricing.

### Present the three-point anchor

Every pitch ends with **three numbers, not one:**

1. **Opening price** (what they pay if they wait).
2. **Standard founder price** (what anyone else on this list gets).
3. **Call-exclusive price** (what I can do for you right now because we're on the phone).

The first two prices exist to make the third one feel earned. **Never lead with the lowest number.**

### Recommend, don't offer

When it's time to pick a tier, make a recommendation based on what they told you:

> "Based on what you said — you're replacing your current gym routine, you want community, and you're looking at 3–4 times a week — I'd genuinely recommend the unlimited. Here's why. [Reiterate their words back.] Are you opposed to that option?"

The question at the end is intentional — it's easier to not be opposed than to agree. **It's the lowest-friction yes.**

---

## E — Execute

Close the sale. The close is a question, not a statement.

### The magic question

> **"Are you opposed to getting set up today?"**

Why this works: it's unusually phrased, which disrupts the automatic "let me think about it." It asks permission to stop selling and start processing. The answer is almost always either "yes, let's do it" or a surfaceable objection you can handle.

### Alternate closes

- "Would it not make sense to get locked in at this rate today?"
- "Can I go ahead and get your info so I can get you set up?"
- "Let's lock this in — I'll need your first name, last name, and email."

### Close the notes

When the lead says yes, **stop talking about the product.** Get the card. Don't re-pitch. Don't re-justify. Don't introduce new features. Silence is your friend here; one more feature can talk them out of it.

---

## Running the DRIVE as a Diagnostic

When a call stalls, run the DRIVE backward. Which step did you skip?

- Stalled at price? → You skipped **R** (upfront contract).
- They're shopping you? → You skipped **I** (why chain).
- They won't commit? → You skipped the **recommendation in V**.
- They keep asking you to email info? → You skipped **E** (didn't actually ask to close).

### Coaching shorthand

Your manager will review calls with you. The common phrases you'll hear:

- **"Run the DRIVE."** — Pitch it to me start to finish.
- **"Which step did you miss?"** — Self-diagnose before I tell you.
- **"Sound like the expert."** — You're apologizing. Stop.
