You do not become a good closer by reading this document. You become a good closer by **repping the pitch against realistic customers** until the words are automatic and the improvisation is conscious.

Revryze has a two-phase certification path: a **generic certification** (brand-agnostic) that every rep passes, and a **brand-specific certification** for each client in your book.

---

## Phase 1: Generic Certification

Before you take your first live call, you must pass four practice drills with your pod leader. Use the scripts below and substitute `[CLIENT]` with whatever brand you're practicing.

### Drill 1: The Cold Call

You call; your pod leader is a lead who filled out a form two days ago. They will play a lead who is: busy, skeptical, mildly interested, and price-sensitive.

**Pass criteria:**

- Deliver the canonical opener without reading.
- Upfront contract (R) in the first 60 seconds.
- At least three Investigate questions, including the magic **"what initially got you interested?"**
- Three-point price anchor.
- Close with **"are you opposed to getting set up today?"**

### Drill 2: Objection Circle

You and your pod leader sit across from each other. They throw an objection; you handle it. You throw one back; they handle it. Ten rounds, no pauses longer than five seconds.

Objections pulled from the master list:

- Spousal buy-in.
- Price-too-high.
- "I need to think about it."
- Competitor comparison.
- "You're not open yet."
- "Can I try it first?"
- "Call me back."
- "It's not for me."
- "I don't have time."
- Hostile / pushy lead.

**Pass criteria:**

- **No "I totally understand."**
- At least one "Does it not make sense...?" / "Is it not worth...?" / "Are you opposed to...?" deployed.
- A takeaway appears at least once.
- You surface the objection behind the objection on at least three rounds.

### Drill 3: The Full Pitch with Adversarial Customer

Your pod leader plays one of the canonical hard customers. Pick one, don't tell the rep which.

#### The Canonical Customer Archetypes

1. **The Bodybuilder on Recovery:** big guy, lifts heavy, curious about Pilates for recovery only. Wants to discount the unlimited and buy 4-session only.
2. **The Club Pilates Defector:** already attends a competitor four times a week. Likes Pilates; skeptical of switching.
3. **The Mother-Daughter Split:** wants to sign up with her daughter. Needs the referral stack to work.
4. **The Hesitant Holder:** says yes to everything but won't close. Needs the fresh-voice handoff.
5. **The Marathon Runner:** ran her last marathon in 3:15, now rehabbing a knee. Wants recovery-tier pricing. Needs the takeaway.
6. **The Post-Weight-Loss Woman:** lost 80 lbs after kids. Emotionally loaded; needs empathy plus a firm close.
7. **The British Ex-Chicago Transplant:** moved to California, thinks everything is priced wrong. Price-anchor against her Chicago gym.
8. **The Low Back Pain Lead:** doctor told her to do Pilates. Soft close — medical weight closes this one.
9. **The CrossFit Dropout:** bored of CrossFit, curious but snobby. Sell strength + community.
10. **The 600-Lb Life Guy:** dramatic weight-loss goals, nervous about group classes. Sell community + zero-risk + grace period.
11. **The Sugar-Daddy Solidcore Girlfriend:** partner pays for everything. Needs the partner-stack close.

**Pass criteria:**

- Complete DRIVE execution start to finish.
- Appropriate tier recommendation based on what the "lead" said.
- At least one objection fully handled with a canonical move.
- You either close or you hit a clean, professional takeaway.

### Drill 4: Self-Listen Review

Pull three of your own recorded calls (two losses, one win). Review each with your pod leader. Identify for each:

- Which DRIVE step you executed well.
- Which DRIVE step you skipped or rushed.
- One specific thing you'll do differently on the next live call.

**Pass criteria:** you can articulate your own gaps without your pod leader telling you.

## Phase 2: Brand-Specific Certification

After generic cert, you're assigned to your first brand. Before you take live calls on that brand, pass these:

1. Recite the nine Product Knowledge data points from memory (see Module 3).
2. Deliver the brand's opening pitch without notes.
3. Run through the checkout flow end-to-end on a test lead.
4. Roleplay two objections that are brand-specific (your pod leader will pick).
5. Handle a "what's different about you vs [Competitor]" question with the brand's actual UVPs.

When a new brand is added to your pod mid-engagement, the brand-specific cert compresses to a 30-minute session and the first-day production expectation is halved.

## Ongoing Practice Rhythm

After certification, you're not done. The weekly practice rhythm:

- **Friday 1-1 with pod leader (30 min):** half pitch practice, half objection circle. Not performance review — pitch practice.
- **Weekly objection circle (group, 15 min):** one objection per rep, rotate.
- **Monthly call review (team, 30 min):** one rep brings a recorded call they lost; team diagnoses together.
- **Self-review (ongoing):** listen to at least three of your own calls a week.

## Coaching Language You'll Hear

When your manager reviews a call with you, these are the shorthand phrases:

- **"Run the DRIVE."** — walk me through the call end-to-end.
- **"Which step did you miss?"** — self-diagnose before I tell you.
- **"Sound like the expert."** — stop apologizing.
- **"Reserve the close."** — you skipped the upfront contract.
- **"What's their why?"** — you didn't investigate deeply enough.
- **"Use their words."** — the close should reference what they actually said earlier.
- **"Hold the silence."** — you filled a pause they were about to break.

## Growth Mindset

Three things to hold close:

1. **Embarrassing yourself is the learning mechanism.** First call is awkward. Tenth is less awkward. Hundredth is automatic. Listen to your worst calls — that's where the next improvement lives.
2. **Confidence is a skill, not a trait.** Walk into the call as the expert with inventory to allocate. The lead is lucky you're calling. They filled out a form. You have what they asked for. Sound like all of that.
3. **We scale each other's pocketbooks.** Share the techniques that work. Shout out the handles that close. When one rep on the team gets better, the whole team earns more — the fulfillment bonus pool is a team pool.

## Final north star

> Sales is communication. You're not pushing, you're matching — matching their stated goals to our actual offer, matching their emotional weight to our value proposition, matching their price comfort to our tier structure. If the match is real, the sale closes itself. Your job is to find the match and get out of the way.
