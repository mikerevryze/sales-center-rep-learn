This module covers what a good day looks like. Hit these targets consistently and the compensation math takes care of itself.

## Weekly Target

**25 memberships per rep per week** across your assigned locations. Some weeks you'll hit 30. Some weeks you'll hit 14. Both are normal. What matters is the rolling four-week average.

The calendar week runs Sunday through Saturday.

## Ramp Expectations

You will not hit 25 in week one. Here is a realistic ramp:

- **Week 1:** 7–11 sales. The goal is to close anyone at all and build the reps.
- **Week 2:** 10–16 sales. You should be cold-calling (about 40% of your dials) in addition to working book calls.
- **Week 3–4:** climbing toward 20.
- **Week 6–8:** 25+ becomes routine. The pitch starts to feel mindless; you're executing without thinking about it.

Your first week will be awkward. Your first call will be awkward. Your second call will be slightly less awkward. This is how it always goes. Don't let week-one discomfort convince you you're in the wrong job.

## Daily Activity Standards

### The 11 AM Rule

Your **New Leads section must be empty by 11 AM every day.** That means every new lead that came in overnight or earlier that morning has been dialed at least once and texted at least once.

This beats a raw dial count as a standard because it forces the right behavior: speed-to-lead on fresh leads is the single highest-leverage action you can take.

### Dials

- Hit your account minimum touch point (every live lead gets at least one dial per day until they qualify or close-lost).
- **Time-zone aware:** Eastern leads get called at 9 AM local; Central at 10–11 local; Pacific not before 10 AM local on weekdays.
- Rotate which pipeline and which time slot you power-dial day to day. Variety moves stuck leads.

### Texting

- Check your SMS inbox every hour.
- **Zero unread at end of day — no exceptions.**
- Be the last person to send. If a lead says "no thanks," send them a polite closing text anyway. Then mark read and move on. It's the last-laugh rule.
- Every text needs urgency and a call-to-action. **"How do you want to proceed?"** is the all-purpose utility text.

### Voicemails

- **Never leave a voicemail manually.** Counterintuitive but true: people are more likely to call back a missed call with no voicemail than a missed call with one. Use VM drops (automation) at end of day to batch-drop the day's no-answers.

## Weekend & Evening Coverage

- Weekend calling is optional. Max 2–3 hours.
- Earliest weekend call: 9 AM local time. 10 AM is the sweet spot.
- Weekend volume: appointment-booking lifts more than sales. Expect more bookings, not more closes.
- Weekday evening cutoff for Pacific calls: 7 PM their time. Ask yourself: would I want this call right now? If no, don't.
- Text-back pattern: people text back more on weekdays than on weekends. Don't expect Saturday mass texts to perform like Tuesday ones.

## Environment & Presentation

- **No background noise.** Silence your phone, shut the TV, no TikTok audio.
- **Look good, smell good, sound good.** You sound more confident when you're put together — leads can hear it.
- Subscribe to your pod's Google Calendars so you know who's in meetings and who's available for tag-teaming a sticky lead.

## Lunches, Walks, and PTO

- **Take your lunch.** Shortening it to grind more hours is actively discouraged. If you're asked to cover a second brand through lunch, push back — someone else needs to cover.
- **Walks are encouraged.** Your pod leader walks to the gas station twice a day. You can too. A 15-minute walk between power-dialing sessions clears your head and resets your energy.
- **Time off** is earned flexibility, not formal PTO (yet). Need to log off early or log on late for a personal thing? Tell your manager ahead of time and work around it. Communication first.

## Confidence Curve

Every new rep follows roughly the same confidence curve:

- **First 2 weeks:** nervous. Reading your notes. Apologizing on the phone.
- **Weeks 3–4:** five clicks less nervous. You know the pitch. You're still in your head.
- **Weeks 5–6:** ten clicks less nervous. You start asking real questions. You start disagreeing with leads.
- **Weeks 6–8:** the 10–50 zone. You're hitting numbers. You start experimenting.
- **Week 8+:** ceiling. The pitch is automatic. You're coaching newer reps. You're eligible for pod leader consideration.

### Self-talk that works

- Before a hard call: _"I'm the expert on this. They're the one who filled out the form. They're lucky I'm calling right now."_
- After a rough call: _"OK, what question did I miss? What did I learn?"_ Then get back on the phone inside 60 seconds.

## Listen to Your Own Calls

GHL records every call. **At least once a week, listen to three of your own calls — one win, two losses.** You will hear things you didn't know you said. Filler words. Dropped questions. Moments of silence you should have held. Moments of silence you should have filled. This is the primary feedback loop for pitch improvement.

It will be embarrassing. Most people hate their own voice. That embarrassment is where the improvement comes from.
