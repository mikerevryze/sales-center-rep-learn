Revryze onboards new brands continuously. Memorizing a single brand's menu is not a career skill — knowing how to learn any brand is. This module teaches the framework.

When you get assigned to a new brand, your pod leader will drop a single onboarding message in your inbox: doc links, login credentials, checkout access, an invitation to pitch them the brand first, and an offer to pitch you back. That's the whole onboarding process. Your job is to convert that into a working pitch **inside 72 hours.**

## The Nine Questions You Must Answer for Every Brand

Whatever the brand, your pitch needs these nine data points. Pull them from the client's site, the onboarding docs, the SOP wiki, and a practice pitch with your manager.

1. **Membership tiers and monthly prices.** (Opening price, standard founder price, and the lowest call-exclusive rate you can quote.)
2. **Session counts per tier.** (Unlimited, 8-session, 4-session, drop-in, etc.)
3. **Unique value proposition.** (What does this brand do that its nearest competitor doesn't?)
4. **Nearest competitor, and why their customers are leaving.**
5. **Grace-period refund policy.** (Days post-open during which a founder can get a full refund.)
6. **Cancellation policy after grace period.** (30-day notice, no rollover, etc.)
7. **Referral / partner discount mechanic.** (Common pattern: $25 off initial fee if you bring in a partner, applied to both memberships.)
8. **Typical demographic.** (Age range, income bracket, lifestyle — so you can adapt tone and references.)
9. **Opening date, and how firm it is.** (If it keeps slipping, use "our development team is targeting mid-[month]" instead of a specific date.)

## The Founding Membership Pattern

Almost every Revryze brand uses the same founding-membership architecture. Learn this once; it carries between brands.

- Charged **one time, before opening.** No recurring billing starts until opening.
- **No contract. No enrollment fee. No sales tax** on the initial founder charge.
- **Grace-period refund** after opening: if they hate it, they get that first charge refunded in full. **Zero risk.**
- First monthly charge typically lands **30 days after the location opens,** not 30 days after sign-up.
- Founder members get priority booking, locked-in pricing for the life of the membership, and grab-bag perks (merch, events). **Downplay the merch — no one signs up for a t-shirt.**

## The Tier System (Universal)

Revryze brands use a three-tier pricing structure that creates urgency independent of the opening date:

- **Tier 1:** the cheapest early-bird pricing. Fills fast.
- **Tier 2:** fills in the middle weeks.
- **Tier 3:** standard founder pricing. Available up to opening.

When tier 1 sells out, tier 2 becomes the entry-level founder rate, and the tier 3 price becomes the new top. **Leads who sit on the fence pay more. Leads who close fast pay less.** This is what you communicate as urgency.

You also have a **call-exclusive discount** below the published tier — roughly $10–20 below the lowest visible price, authorized only if the lead closes on the call.

> **Rule: the website price is always higher than your phone price.** This is intentional. Brands deliberately set the online price above the phone price so that a live conversation with you becomes the cheapest way to sign up. If a lead says "but the website says [higher price]," that's a buying signal — you're now handing them a discount they can only get through you.

## Service-Tiered Brands (Brand-Agnostic Disclosure Pattern)

Some brands (pet grooming, aesthetic services, specialty medical) price by service complexity rather than by frequency. For these brands, disclose the full level 1–3 range up front so nothing comes as a shock:

> "Here's level one through three pricing. Level one is the base rate. Level two is a little more because it takes more time and care. Level three is the premium. We'll assess on arrival to confirm which level applies."

This removes the "bait-and-switch" feeling when the actual service price is quoted later.

## Medical / Specialist Brands

For brands with a licensed specialist component (med spas, clinics), **you are a sales rep, not a clinician.** Specific medical questions get escalated. Your talk track:

> "Great question — let me CC our studio nurse, she'll handle that one directly. In the meantime, let's get your spot reserved."

Keep the sale moving.

## Onboarding Checklist for a New Brand

When a new brand lands in your pod, run this checklist before your first call:

1. Read the onboarding email. Log in to every system listed.
2. Find the nine data points above. Write them in a one-page personal cheat sheet.
3. Pitch your pod leader as if they're a lead. Get feedback on wording and gaps.
4. Take a reverse pitch from your pod leader so you hear the brand's voice.
5. Run through the checkout flow end-to-end on a test account. Know where every field lives before you need it live on a call.
6. Identify the two most likely objections for this brand and draft a handle for each.
7. Identify the five buzzwords a knowledgeable prospect would expect you to use. Google anything you don't know. **You have 15 minutes to be "dangerous" on a new topic.**

## The Expert-Voice Rule

> You don't have to **be** the expert. You just have to **sound like one.** Use the right terminology, ask the right questions, and say "great question — let me get that answer" on anything you don't know instead of guessing. Prospects buy confidence, not credentials.
