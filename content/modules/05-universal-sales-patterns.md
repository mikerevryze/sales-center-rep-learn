These patterns repeat across every brand Revryze runs. Internalize them; deploy them on demand.

## Tier-Driven Urgency

Every Revryze pre-launch has a three-tier pricing structure. As one tier sells out, the next tier becomes the new entry point. Communicate this explicitly:

> "We're in Tier 2 right now. That means the first batch of founders is gone. When we fill up again, the price goes up 35%, not down. So there's no reward for waiting — only cost."

This works because it creates a deadline that is independent of the opening date. **"We're not open yet" stops being a reason to wait when they also know the price only moves one direction.**

## The 50%-Higher Text Framing

When texting about price increases, quantify the jump. **"Price goes up 35%"** or **"your rate will be 50% higher at opening"** sticks harder than "prices will be higher." Specific numbers feel true.

## Hive-Mind Social Proof

"You're not alone" is a canonical Revryze move. When a lead voices a hesitation, confirm that many others share it and walk them through what those others decided. The structure:

1. Name the hesitation out loud (validate).
2. Tell them dozens of other leads had the exact same one.
3. Tell them what those leads did (the social proof path).
4. Pivot back to the close.

> "I've talked to over three dozen people from [Competitor] who said the exact same thing, and that's exactly why they left and came to us. So you're not alone in that sentiment — and if you were to make the switch, you definitely wouldn't be the first."

The underlying principle: **people don't want to be the first, and they don't want to be the last. They want to be with the herd.**

## The Partner / Referral Stack

When a lead mentions a spouse, partner, or friend who also works out, stack the sale:

- Offer a $25 discount off their initial membership fee (or equivalent).
- Extend the same discount to the partner when they sign up.
- Close both at once if possible.

> "Your wife does Pilates — that's awesome. Tell you what: I'll lock you in at [founder rate], throw in $25 off your first month, and when she signs up, she gets the same thing. So it's effectively [lower rate] each. Does that work?"

## The Manager-Ask Theater

When a lead needs one more nudge to close today and a further discount would do it:

1. **Get the buy-in first:** "If I ask my manager right now and get approval to knock another $10 off, will you sign up with me today?"
2. **Only after they say yes:** mute the line, count to 15, come back on.
3. "Alright, she said we can do it. Let's lock it in."

**Critical:** the discount is gated on a same-call signup. "I'm sticking my neck out for this — if I come back with approval and you don't sign up, I look bad. So let's lock the yes first."

> **Discount economics — use sparingly.** Every $10 you discount comes out of your commission on scale. Forty sales at a $10 discount is a $400 hole in your paycheck. Use the call-exclusive price as a last-resort close, not a first-round offer. When you do use it, make the lead feel it cost you something to get.

## Four/Eight-Session as Back-Pocket

Some leads are anti-unlimited and anti-four-session. They want a "try it first" option. Don't advertise the eight-session; back-pocket it.

> "If you're not sure about an unlimited, here's what I can do. I can set you up with a discounted credit plan — eight classes at [price] for the first month. It's essentially a paid trial. If you love it, we upgrade you to unlimited at the founder rate I already quoted. If you don't, your grace-period refund still applies."

The **"discounted credit plan"** reframe matters. Many leads have a mental allergy to "membership" but are fine with "bundle" or "credits." The mechanics are identical; the word matters. **Use their comfort word.**

## Drop-In Pricing Reframe

When a lead asks about drop-in rates, math it for them:

> "Two drop-ins a month is $80. Our four-session founder rate is $89. So for $9 more, you get double the classes and lock your founder rate forever."

**Math beats narrative every time.**

## Manager-Silence for an Extra $10

When a lead is close but the price is the last sticking point:

> "Hold on one second. Let me see what I can do. [mute for 10 seconds] Okay, I can knock another $10 off the monthly. That brings you to [price]. If I get that approved, we're signing up right now, right?"

The silence does the work. Don't fill it.

## Sell Tier 1 by Accident

Sometimes you'll realize mid-call that a lead qualifies for a higher tier than they thought — maybe a referral credit, a founder stacking, or a legacy pricing adjustment.

> "Let me pull that up — actually, it looks like I can get you Tier 1 pricing on that. That saves you an extra $[X] a month."

No one complains when you hand them a better deal than they expected. But make them feel the win — pause, let them register the save, then close.
