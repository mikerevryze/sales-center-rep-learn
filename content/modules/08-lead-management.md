The one-liner for this entire module:

> **Craig's #1 Rule**
>
> Your leads are your book of business. Manage them or someone else will.

Read that again. Internalize it. This is the single most important rule at Revryze. Everything in this module is elaboration on that one sentence.

## Speed to Lead

Fresh leads convert at **10–20x the rate** of 48-hour-old leads. **The New Leads section must be empty by 11 AM every day.**

- **First contact:** one dial and one text, always within 30 minutes of the lead appearing.
- If a lead comes in after hours, it's hit by the automated first-touch text and gets your manual dial + text at 9 AM local the next morning.
- A lead that sat untouched for 48 hours is half as likely to ever answer. A lead untouched for a week is essentially close-lost.

## Book of Business Ownership

Every lead in your pipeline has an owner. The rules:

- **Untouched for 2 days:** still contactable, owner is still you.
- **Untouched for 3+ days:** fair game. Another rep can pick it up if they see an untouched lead in a shared list. **That sale becomes theirs.**
- **Ownership conflicts:** whoever has the conversation history owns the lead. If you've texted back and forth 5–10 times and another rep has never touched it, it's yours — even if the system briefly reassigned. Automation-nurture does **not** count as rep conversation.

## Follow-Up Priority Order

When you sit down at your desk, work this order:

1. **NEW LEAD** — clear by 11 AM.
2. **PITCHED** — these are your highest-intent unclosed leads. Rehash aggressively.
3. **CONNECTED / BRUSHED OFF** — warm follow-up.
4. **NO SHOW** — reschedule.
5. **ATTEMPTING TO CONTACT** — the long tail. Work these in the late afternoon.

For booking-only support roles (coverage reps who just book, don't close), the priority shrinks to NEW LEAD + CONNECTED only.

## Text Rules

- **Every text has urgency and a call to action.**
- **Zero unread at end of day.**
- **Be the last to send.** If they say no, send a brief polite goodbye anyway.
- **Time-zone aware:** 9 AM local on the East Coast before 10 AM Central; 10–11 AM local for West Coast, never before 9.
- When rescheduling, use the phrase **"at your suggested time"** to avoid the false-impression-of-urgency issue.
- The mass-text all-purpose prompt: **"Hey [Name] — how do you want to proceed?"**

## Qualify / Disqualify / Close-Lost Decision

Not every lead is worth your time. Decide quickly:

- **QUALIFY:** any lead who engages, asks questions, or answers the phone. Keep in your book; pitch them.
- **DISQUALIFY:** any lead who explicitly says no or stop, who's clearly a tire-kicker with no means, or who has a disqualifying location/situation.
- **CLOSE LOST:** move immediately once disqualified. Don't let dead leads clog the pipeline.

If a lead is clearly not a fit — wrong location, no budget, already a member — close lost them same day. You're not being rude. You're freeing yourself to work leads who can actually buy.

## Rehashing Pitched Leads

**PITCHED is the most valuable pipeline stage.** These leads heard the full DRIVE pitch and didn't buy. That means they engaged. They just need one more thing — a new angle, a new discount, a new spouse's approval. Hit them on a 48-hour cadence with:

- A fresh-voice call (hand to a teammate or manager).
- A direct text: "How do you want to proceed?"
- A takeaway: "Tier 1 filling up — if you're in, now's the moment."
- A VM drop as part of the weekly mass touch.

## Event Lead Protocol

When a brand runs an event (open house, grand opening preview), leads who show up in person get handled differently:

- **Walk-up sign-ups:** sell on the spot. Stack the founder rate; same rules as the phone.
- **10+ minute conversations:** book them for a phone call later. Standing conversations don't close as well as scheduled calls with full attention.
- **Sign-in sheet leads:** these go into GHL same day; first touch happens next morning.

## "Like But Don't Reply" Leads

Some leads like your social posts, view your DMs, open your texts — but never reply. **Treat them like PITCHED:** they're engaged, they're just lurking. Send a direct text asking what's holding them back. **Don't let silence become your answer.**

## Capacity Communication

If your book is too full to hit the 11 AM empty rule, **tell your pod leader.** They'll redistribute. Hiding a backed-up book hurts the team; so does claiming you can handle more than you can. Communication first.

## Tag-Team Policy

If you and another rep both have a live lead who's stuck, tag-teaming is encouraged.

> "Hey, let me grab my colleague — she's better on this objection than I am."

Commissions split if the sale is a true handoff. Manager arbitrates.

## Moral Obligation to Close

If you see an untouched lead in a shared list — and it's been sitting — you have a moral obligation to reach out. Someone in the company has already paid the ad dollars that generated that lead. **Leaving them cold is leaving money on the floor.**

This is how new reps break out early: they watch the shared pipeline, they see untouched 3-day leads, they dial and close. Within two weeks they're outperforming reps who only work their assigned book.
